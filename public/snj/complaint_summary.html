1a · Specific remedy demand — Pergola

The agreed pergola is the StruXure custom structure — five 20% increments of $38,194.80 = $190,974.00 (~$190k). Separately, S&J charged $18,964.00 for pergola engineering (CRES) but never delivered the engineering work product / IP to Habermeier. Pergola money paid to date: $57,158.80 ($18,964.00 engineering + $38,194.80 structure deposit).

  • If S&J will NOT build the pergola: refund ALL pergola money — the $38,194.80 structure deposit + $18,964.00 engineering + any other pergola prep = $57,158.80, plus 7.44% carrying interest.
  • If S&J builds the pergola at the agreed $190,974.00: for all $18,964.00 of pergola engineering, S&J must deliver the stamped work product / IP and proof of payment to the engineer — or refund/credit the full $18,964.00 plus 7.44% carrying interest. None of that IP was delivered, and engineering belongs within the $190,974.00 structure price, not billed on top.
  • Either way: S&J must deliver the stamped pergola engineering it charged for, or refund it — Habermeier paid for a deliverable he never received (§7159.5(a)(5) / §7108).

1b · Deliver ALL work product Habermeier paid for

Beyond the pergola, Habermeier paid for substantial architectural and engineering work across the project — design and architectural modifications, structural computations, Title 24, permit sets, and engineering. S&J must deliver all of it.

  • Every plan, drawing, structural calculation, architectural set, specification, permit set, and stamped engineering document Habermeier was billed for — in complete, usable form — together with the ownership and/or use rights Habermeier is entitled to.
  • For any such work product billed but not delivered: deliver it, or refund the charge plus 7.44% carrying interest. Habermeier paid for these deliverables and is entitled to them for his own project.

2 · Unfair billing pattern

S&J invoices before value exists, and the cash asymmetry runs only one way — toward S&J.

  • S&J is not permitted to invoice until the work/material is on site or installed (§7159.5(a)(5) — payment may not exceed the value delivered). A more lenient line would be “bill once S&J actually owes a subcontractor or supplier.” S&J's practice is worse than even that: it has invoiced Habermeier for items it has not yet paid anyone for — pure use of Habermeier's cash as float.
  • Overages & additions are invoiced immediately — routinely 50% or 100% “at approval,” before any work. Habermeier is made to front the money ASAP.
  • Credits are back-loaded and on paper. S&J agreed to credit $171,000.00 for scope Habermeier removed (pool, BBQ, closet, fireplace), but only $34,200.00 ever reached a statement; $136,800.00 sits in S&J's internal system / scheduled to the back end, not applied as a real-time offset against what Habermeier pays.
  • Net effect: Habermeier continuously fronts cash on the charge side while the credits he is owed lag on the offset side — and he carries the cost of money on both.
  • S&J was warned in writing and continued anyway. Habermeier's March 11, 2026 letter expressly told S&J that its front-loaded billing violates §7159.5(a)(5). In the March 10 conversation Jim Long claimed “specialty products” are exempt — but no such exemption exists; advance billing is lawful only under a §7159.5(a)(8) bond / joint-control arrangement, which S&J does not have. S&J kept billing ahead regardless — knowing, willful conduct (relevant to B&P §7119 and to any good-faith defense).

3 · Schedule delay & rent

The signed 6232 contract (S&J lic. #993363; start 05-08-2024) states an approximate completion date of 05-31-2025. S&J's current QuickBase projects homeowner occupancy at 11-16-2026 — roughly 17.5 months (534 days) past the contract's own completion date.

  • The move-in date was quietly moved in QuickBase — March → August → November — with no heads-up or conversation (Habermeier's 5/29/2026 email). Silent slippage undercuts any claim that S&J gave contemporaneous notice.
  • S&J attributes the delay to Habermeier's change orders, and the contract hedges that completion “may be influenced by intervening tasks.” That cannot account for ~17.5 months. The burden is on S&J to prove (a) the COs actually caused the delay and (b) that it gave written notice of the time impact at the time. Whatever the cause — cash-flow constraints (including any misuse or diversion of funds collected) or a personnel / labor shortage — it lies with S&J, not with Habermeier. Notably, S&J collects ahead of the work (the float pattern above) yet the work still stalls.
  • Habermeier's carrying cost of the delay is rent: $5,300.00/month ($1,325.00/week). Conservative 9-month floor = $47,700.00; on the full ~17.5-month slip ≈ $92,750.00, and still growing weekly.
  • There is no delay or consequential-damages-waiver clause — and that cuts in Habermeier's favor. Its absence means the rent (a foreseeable cost of the delay) is not waived. No contract can license a contractor to take two or three times a reasonable time: the contract fixed an approximate completion of 05-31-2025; California law independently requires performance within a reasonable time (Civ. Code §1657); and willful failure to prosecute the work with reasonable diligence is itself a CSLB violation (B&P §7119). A ~17.5-month overrun is facially unreasonable. (Analysis, not legal advice — confirm before filing.)
