This letter follows our letters of March 11, 2026 and May 26, 2026, both delivered by registered mail (the May 26 letter also by email, with response deadlines of May 29 and June 2, 2026). S&J did not adequately respond to or cure the matters raised in those letters. This is therefore a formal notice that S&J Properties Construction, Inc. (“S&J”) is in material breach of the 6232 contract for 270 Bolla Ave, and a final demand that S&J cure the defaults below within ten (10) days of the date of this letter. Because S&J has had notice of these matters since March and detailed written demands since May 26, ten days is a reasonable time to cure. A cure requires S&J to actually perform the items below within that period — a promise or a plan to perform is not a cure.

A. The defaults

  1. Collecting payment ahead of value, and not applying funds to their stated purpose. S&J has repeatedly billed and collected in full before the work or materials existed — in several instances the vendor or subcontractor received nothing — contrary to Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §7159.5(a)(5) (no payment beyond the value of work performed or materials delivered) and §7108 (diversion of funds). On information and belief, S&J has invoiced for items it had not yet paid anyone for. S&J was put on written notice of this on March 11, 2026; it claimed “specialty products” are exempt — no such exemption exists (advance billing is permitted only under a §7159.5(a)(8) bond/joint-control arrangement S&J does not have) — and continued billing ahead regardless.
  2. Failure to pass through agreed credits — and billing progress against removed scope. S&J agreed to credit $171,000.00 for scope removed by Habermeier (pool, BBQ, closet system, fireplace), recorded internally as the “Credit Selection Groups.” Only a fraction has appeared on a statement; approximately $68,400.00 is marked “Completed” in S&J's QuickBase but was never credited to Habermeier on any statement, and the remainder is back-loaded. Worse, the de-scope sits inside the base the percentage milestones bill against, yet S&J bills those milestones against inconsistent bases — including the full pre-de-scope base of $1,645,350.00 as recently as 2026-04-23 — so Habermeier is charged progress on scope that no longer exists, rather than the credit being applied against each milestone as it is billed (§7159.5(a)(5)).
  3. Pergola engineering and structure. S&J charged Habermeier $18,964.00 for pergola engineering but never delivered the engineering work product / intellectual property, and has collected a deposit on the ~$190,974.00 custom structure without confirming it will build it.
  4. Undisclosed subcontractors. S&J used at least one subcontractor never disclosed in the contract (a Mi Casa Construction crew, CSLB #978409, observed performing framing and prep specifically for the LaCantina door installation on site), contrary to §7159(c)(7).
  5. Unlawful down payment. S&J collected $174,650.00 at signing, far exceeding the statutory cap of the lesser of $1,000 or 10% (§7159(d)(8)).
  6. Failure to prosecute the work with reasonable diligence. The contract's approximate completion date is 05-31-2025; S&J now projects occupancy on or about 11-16-2026 — roughly 17.5 months late — and the move-in date has been changed repeatedly in S&J's system without contemporaneous written notice. This is contrary to the duty to perform within a reasonable time (Civ. Code §1657) and to prosecute the work with reasonable diligence (B&P §7119).
  7. Failure to furnish lien releases; subcontractor non-payment. S&J has not furnished lien releases for amounts already paid (§7159.5(a)(6)), and the roofing subcontractor (Cobex Construction Group) served a $120,057 California preliminary notice on 05-21-2026 after S&J failed to pay it — exposing the property to a mechanic's lien.

B. What S&J must do to cure (within 10 days)

To the extent any item below is already satisfied, provide the documentation that shows it. For Habermeier to consider the breach cured, S&J must:

Two points apply throughout: (i) the measure of value is delivery of materials to the site, or installation/completion of the work (§7159.5(a)(5)) — not merely that S&J paid a third party; and (ii) wherever a refund or credit is owed, interest accrues at 7.44% from the date S&J received Habermeier's funds until the date value is delivered on site (or the money is refunded).

  1. Apply the full $171,000.00 de-scope credit on a corrected, written statement — including the ~$68,400.00 recorded “Completed” but never credited. Going forward, bill every percentage milestone against one consistent post-de-scope base ($1,474,350.00), and provide a single running de-scope-credit account showing, at each milestone, the credit released versus the proportional amount owed (% billed × $171,000.00) — so the credit is applied in real time, not on S&J's discretionary schedule.
  2. For every charge collected ahead of value: provide proof that the materials were delivered to the site or the work was installed/completed, with dates. Absent that, refund or credit the amount plus 7.44% interest, computed from the date S&J received the funds to the date of refund or delivery.
  3. For every “needs proof” item: prove the item was delivered to the site or installed, with dates; absent that proof, refund or credit plus interest on the same basis.
  4. Pergola: deliver the stamped pergola engineering work product / IP for all $18,964.00 of engineering charges, with proof of payment to the engineer — or refund/credit the full $18,964.00 plus 7.44% interest. Confirm in writing whether S&J will build the pergola at the agreed ~$190,974.00; if not, refund all pergola money paid (≈ $57,158.80) plus interest.
  5. Deliver ALL design and engineering work product Habermeier was billed for — every plan, drawing, structural computation, architectural set, Title 24, specification, permit set, and stamped engineering document — in complete, usable form, together with the ownership and/or use rights Habermeier is entitled to. For any such work product billed but not delivered, deliver it or refund the charge plus 7.44% carrying interest.
  6. Furnish lien releases (§7159.5(a)(6)) for every amount paid to date — conditional or unconditional waivers from each subcontractor and supplier (concrete, framing, roofing, plumbing, electrical, stucco) — and resolve the Cobex $120,057 preliminary notice with proof of payment and a lien release; furnish a release before each future payment.
  7. Disclose, in writing, every subcontractor and material supplier used on the project — legal name, CSLB license number, scope, bid/contract, and invoices. This includes those named in the May 26 letter (Villas Concrete; Juarez Framing; Cobex Construction; EROD plaster/stucco; SW Cabinets; California Marble & Stone; the plumbing, HVAC, and electrical trades; and the pergola, front-door, and garage-door suppliers). The framing subcontractor appears to be Mi Casa Construction / Rolando Juarez (CSLB #978409), which was never disclosed in the contract.
  8. Provide a complete, reconciled accounting that ties S&J's statements to its QuickBase ledger, and correct the down-payment overage.
  9. Provide a realistic written completion schedule, and either document, with contemporaneous records, that change orders caused the delay (and that S&J gave written notice of the time impact at the time) or address Habermeier's delay damages (rent at $5,300.00/month).
  10. Going forward, bill only per the invoicing rules in the May 26, 2026 letter — payment only for work completed on site, materials delivered to the premises, or off-site materials verified under the warehouse protocol, with a lien release before each payment (§§7159(d)(9), 7159.5(a)(5) & (a)(6)).
  11. Site safety — S&J is responsible for the people it hires. The pool safety gate must be kept closed and locked at all times. S&J's subcontractors have repeatedly left it open; controlling its own workers so they do not create a safety hazard or CC&R/liability exposure is S&J's responsibility, not Habermeier's.
